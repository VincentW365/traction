01
Front desk

Arrivals & the counter

The busiest screen in the building. Everything here is built for speed: one search box that takes a phone number, a licence plate or a name, and buttons big enough to hit without looking twice.

Check in a customer who has an appointment

Operations › Check-In

  1. Type the phone number, licence plate or name into the search box and press Enter.
  2. Pick the customer from Search Results. Their booked appointment appears with it.
  3. Check the vehicle is the one in front of you. If they have brought a different car, pick the right vehicle or add it.
  4. Fill in Intake Details — mileage, and the oil brand and grade if they have a preference (Castrol, Valvoline, Amsoil, Liqui Moly, or type your own).
  5. Confirm the vehicle's Vehicle Weight: Light, Standard or Heavy. This decides which bays can take it, so getting it wrong can send the car to a position that cannot lift it.
  6. Press the check-in button. A Ticket Number is issued and an Estimated Start is shown.
  7. Read the estimated start to the customer, and offer them the Customer Status Link.
Two numbers, and only one is the customer's. The big number on screen is the short one, like B01-003 — that is what the waiting-room board shows and what the customer should write down. The longer Reference underneath is for your own enquiries later. Quote the short one at the counter.
On time matters. An appointment checked in within its protected window keeps its place ahead of the walk-ins. Late, and it joins the queue by arrival time like everyone else.

Register a walk-in

Operations › Check-In

  1. Search by phone or plate first — most walk-ins have been here before, and a found record saves you the whole form and keeps their history in one place.
  2. If there is genuinely no record, press Create New Customer and take name, mobile and the vehicle.
  3. Under Walk-In Service Selection, search the service by name, family or code and tick what they are asking for.
  4. Fill in mileage, oil preference and Vehicle Color — the colour is what lets you find the right car in a full yard.
  5. Set Vehicle Weight, then check in. The ticket number is issued and the customer takes their place in the queue by arrival time.
Never invent an email address. Ask for it, but do not type N/A, a dash or a made-up address to get past the field — the platform treats a placeholder as a real address and the customer's messages go nowhere. Blank is honest and harmless.

Book an appointment for someone on the phone

Bookings › Appointments › New Appointment

Six short steps, each one a single question:

  1. Customer — search, or create a new record.
  2. Vehicle — pick theirs, or add it.
  3. Service request — what they need.
  4. Date.
  5. Available time — what the workshop can take that day.
  6. Review — read it back to the customer, then confirm.
If the day is full you get a warning, not a wall. You can still book — the warning is there so you make that choice deliberately, and it is recorded.
A staff booking does not need an email address. Only the customer's own online booking does. If they have no email, book them anyway and tell them you will call.

Give a customer their live status link

Check-In, or Queue Board › any ticket

  1. Open the ticket and find Customer Status Link. It is also on the check-in confirmation screen.
  2. Press Email to Customer. The email opens with the link already in it, to the address on file; check it and send. Or Copy it to text or hand over.

The email is recorded under Customer Communication like any other. If the customer has no email address on file, the button tells you so — add one or copy the link instead. A link lasts 24 hours from check-in; after that the button is withdrawn rather than sending a dead link.

They then watch their own vehicle move through four stages — Checked In, In Queue, In Service, Ready — with no login and without seeing anybody else's details. It is the single best way to stop “is it ready yet?” calls.

Email a customer

Queue Board › ticket › Customer Communication

  1. Press Email Customer.
  2. Choose the message you need — ready for collection, something needed from the customer, a delay, or vehicle due for service.
  3. The wording is filled in from the template. Read it. Edit anything that does not fit this customer before you send.
  4. Send. The attempt is recorded on the customer's profile and on the visit, whether it succeeded or failed.
Email is the only channel. There is no SMS. If a customer has no email address on file the platform tells you so and stops — add a real address or pick up the phone. Do not fabricate one to force the message through.

Say whether parts are ready

Queue Board › ticket › Parts Readiness

Parts and labour live on your existing systems. What this platform needs from you is one honest answer: are the parts for this job here or not?

  1. Open the ticket and find Parts Readiness.
  2. Tick Acknowledge parts prepositioned once the parts are physically on hand.
Until that is ticked the job is Blocked and will not be handed to a technician. That is deliberate — it stops a car taking a bay it cannot leave. Never tick it to get a job moving if the parts are not actually there.

Move someone up the queue

Queue Board › ticket › Set Queue Override

  1. Open the ticket and press Set Queue Override.
  2. Give the position and the reason. The reason is required.
  3. The ticket shows Override Active from then on, and stays pinned where you put it.
Overrides are recorded against your name. Use them for real reasons — a stranded customer, a vehicle blocking a bay — and expect to be able to explain them.

Handle a no-show or an abandoned vehicle

Queue Board › ticket

  1. Try the customer first, and log the call on the ticket.
  2. If there is no answer and they are not coming, press Mark No-Show / Abandoned and give the reason.

The ticket leaves the live queue, the position is freed, and the record stays so the pattern shows up in reporting later.

Close a visit

Queue Board › ticket

  1. Check the work is finished and quality-checked, and enter Mileage Out.
  2. Press Close Visit.
A visit left open sits in the queue and in the reports for ever, and quietly consumes a slot in tomorrow's availability. Close them the same day.

Log a complaint

Bookings › Complaints › New

  1. Record the customer, the subject and what happened, in their words where you can.
  2. Set the severity. Every severity carries a response deadline — the severity decides how long you have.
  3. Acknowledge it so the customer knows it was received, then work it to Resolved.
SeverityRespond within
Critical4 hours
High24 hours
Medium72 hours
Low120 hours
Past the deadline the complaint shows as Overdue on the list. A manager can change these hours in Settings, so check them against what your shop has agreed. Changing a complaint's severity recalculates the deadline from when it was logged, never from now — you cannot buy time by downgrading it.
02
Everyone

The queue

The Queue Board is the shared picture of the day. Everyone can see it, it refreshes itself every five seconds, and nobody needs to ask anybody else what is going on.

How the order is decided

Operations › Queue Board

The queue is arrival order first. It is not a scoring system, and nobody's place is quietly recalculated. Four things, in this order, decide the board:

  1. Arrival order First checked in, first served. This is the default and it covers most of the day.
  2. On-time appointments jump ahead An appointment checked in inside its protected window goes in front of the walk-ins. That is the whole value of booking. Arrive late and the protection is gone.
  3. A manager override pins a position Set deliberately, with a reason, by a person. It stays until it is removed.
  4. Blocked work steps aside Missing parts, no qualified technician, no suitable bay — the ticket keeps its number but stands out of the way until the block clears. It does not lose its place in line.
The ticket number is not the running order. A ticket number never changes and is what you say to a customer — the short one, the same number the waiting-room board shows. The position on the board moves with the day. Read the position, quote the number.

Read the board

Operations › Queue Board

Each row carries the Ticket, the customer and vehicle, the Services asked for, the Source (appointment or walk-in), the Status, and how long they have been Waiting.

  • Labels such as fast-track, late or missed are descriptions, not instructions. They tell you what happened; they do not move anyone.
  • Blocked means the job cannot start yet. Open it and the reason is on the ticket.
  • Click any row to open the full ticket, which is where almost every front-desk action lives.
03
Technician

On the tools

One screen, one list, big buttons. The platform asks you for as little admin as it can get away with: start, stop, finished. Everything else is worked out from those three.

Find your work

Technician › My Jobs

The screen answers two questions, kept apart on purpose:

  • My Workday — how your day is going. Time on the clock, jobs Completed Today.
  • My Jobs — what to work on now and next.

Your jobs are always in the same order, so your eye learns it: Working → Up Next → Waiting → Rework → Awaiting QC → Completed. The registration number leads every card, because that is what you are looking for in the yard.

Start a job

My Jobs › the card

  1. Find the card, check the registration matches the vehicle in front of you.
  2. Press Start Work.

Your clock starts at that moment. The card moves to Working and the customer's status page now reads In Service.

You can only have one job running at a time. If you try to start a second, the platform stops you and asks what happened to the first. That is what keeps job times honest — two clocks running at once means neither figure is true.

Stop for a hold-up

My Jobs › the working card

  1. Press Pause / Hold-up.
  2. Pick the reason. For a missing part, choose the parts reason and the button becomes Mark Waiting for Parts; otherwise press Confirm Pause.
  3. Add a note if there is anything the advisor needs to know.

The clock stops the moment you confirm — from your timestamp, not from when somebody gets round to acknowledging it. An advisor or manager then acknowledges the stop. Their acknowledgement does not change your time.

Pausing honestly is always the right call. The time is held against the hold-up, not against you, and it is the only way the shop can see where its day actually goes.

Get back on a job

My Jobs › the waiting card

  1. Press Resume Work. If it was waiting on a part, the button reads Parts Arrived — Resume.
  2. If you have another job running, finish or pause that one first — the same one-at-a-time rule applies on resume.

To move back to a job you stepped away from, use Back to this job on its card and say why you are switching.

Finish a job

My Jobs › the working card

  1. Press Finished → QC.

Your clock stops and the job moves to Awaiting QC. It is not closed and the customer is not told it is ready — an advisor or manager does the quality check first.

Pick up rework

My Jobs › Rework

If a quality check sends work back, it reappears in your Rework group with what needs doing. Start it the same way as any other job. The original work and times are kept — rework is recorded alongside them, not written over them.

Record an inspection

My Jobs › the job › Records for this visit

Everything on the visit is in one box on your job: the Job Sheet with where it stands (Start, Finish, or Signed), every inspection already on the visit, and Full visit record for the whole history.

  1. Press + Pre-Inspection or + Vehicle Inspection. The record is created for this car and opens straight away. If one is already in progress the button says Continue instead — finish that one rather than starting a second. (The front desk can also start one from Operations › Inspections.)
  2. Work down the checklist. Anything that does not apply can be marked Skipped — say so rather than leaving it blank.
  3. On the pre-inspection, add photographs where they tell the story better than words. Press Take Photo and the tablet's camera opens on screen — tap the shutter, then Use Photo if it is clear or Retake if it is not. Nothing is saved until you choose. Upload Image takes one already on the device instead.
  4. Sign off. The record then shows as Signed off and cannot be quietly edited.
  5. Press ← Back to My Job at the top. It takes you to the job you opened this from — not to the general Inspections list — and asks first if you have unsaved ticks.
  6. The job sheet works the same way: press Finish Job Sheet on the job, fill it in, and press Save & Done to save and come straight back. When the customer signs it, the job shows Job Sheet — Signed.
The camera needs the secure address. If the tablet is on a plain http:// address, the browser will not open the live camera and Take Photo falls back to the device's own camera app or gallery. Use the https:// address your manager gave you.
A saved draft is yours to come back to. Only a signed-off inspection counts as a finding you can show a customer.

Report something, without editing records

Any vehicle you are working on

You have read-only access to a vehicle's history, which is there so you can see what was done last time. If something in the record is wrong, do not try to correct it — add a note on the job saying what you found. An advisor or manager makes the correction, and the trail shows where the information came from.

Dispatch & the floor

The Dispatch Board is live resource control — who is working, which bays are free, what is stuck, what is waiting on a quality check. It is the screen to leave open on the second monitor.

Read the dispatch board

Operations › Dispatch

Send the next vehicle in

Dispatch › Call Next

  1. Press Call Next.

The platform takes the next eligible ticket in queue order — skipping anything blocked — and puts it in a bay that suits the vehicle and the work. You do not have to pick; that is the point. Use it as the default way to pull work onto the floor.

Assign a technician yourself

Dispatch › Needs Dispatch › Assign

  1. Press Assign on the card. The full ticket opens.
  2. Choose the technician. If they do not hold a skill the services need, the assignment is refused and the message names the missing skill. That is the platform working, not a fault.
  3. Choose the bay, or leave it and the platform takes the first compatible free one. Naming a bay whose type does not match the work is refused the same way.
  4. For a visit with several orders you can give them all to one technician in one action.

To move a job already underway, use Reassign technician on the ticket. Work already done stays credited to the technician who did it.

Absence is shown, not enforced. Marking someone absent in Availability puts them on this board as absent and raises it on the manager's dashboard — but it does not stop you assigning work to them. Check the board before you assign; the platform will not catch this one for you.

Acknowledge a technician's pause

Dispatch › Pause Approval Required

  1. Read who stopped, why, and since when.
  2. Press Approve to acknowledge it.
  3. Press Reject only if the work should not have stopped. A reason is required and the work resumes.
Acknowledging never moves the clock. The stop has been counted from the technician's own timestamp since the moment they confirmed it, so there is no rush and no advantage in being slow. The original request is kept either way.

Do the quality check and finish the order

Queue Board › ticket

A job moves through fixed stages, and the button on the ticket always says what pressing it actually does:

StageWhat you can do
AssignedNothing here — the technician starts it.
In progressMark Work Complete, or park it as waiting for parts or waiting on the customer.
WaitingResume Work once the hold-up clears.
Quality checkComplete Quality Check when the work passes. If it does not, open rework instead.
Ready to invoiceClose Order.
Only once the quality check is complete should the customer be told the vehicle is ready. Sending that email earlier is how a car gets collected mid-job.

Change what a job covers

Queue Board › ticket › Edit services

The platform keeps three different things apart on purpose: what the customer asked for, what was approved, and what was actually done. When the customer approves extra work, add it to the order rather than editing the original request — so the record still shows what they came in for.

06
Front desk & manager

What customers see

Four things reach customers. Knowing exactly what is on each one saves a lot of explaining at the counter.

Online booking

/book — no login

Five plain steps: Let's find you → What do you need? → Pick a time → A few details → Please confirm. Nothing is held until they confirm, and only times the workshop can genuinely take are shown.

  • They identify themselves with their mobile number plus licence plate. New customers enter the same two things and a record is created.
  • What they tell the booking form goes straight onto the job sheet, so they are not asked again on arrival.
  • Online booking does require an email address, because the confirmation and the manage link have nowhere else to go. Booking at the desk does not.

The manage link in their confirmation

Sent with the booking confirmation email

Their confirmation email carries a private link that lets them reschedule or cancel on their own, up to the cutoff you set in Settings. It is single-purpose and expires — it is not an account and it shows them nothing but their own booking.

If a customer says the link does not work, it has most likely expired or already been used. Make the change for them at the desk.

The waiting-room screen

Settings › Workshop Operations › Customer display board

  1. Get the display address from Settings › Workshop Operations › Customer display board.
  2. Open it on the waiting-room screen and leave it. No login, and it refreshes itself.

It shows three columns — Waiting, In Service, Ready — and each vehicle leads with its licence plate, drawn as a plate on one line, with the short ticket number beside it, because a customer recognises their car before they remember a number. Under the plate, Est. start and Est. completion sit side by side in the same place on every card; a car being checked shows as Quality Check. A time appears only when the platform has one (otherwise “to follow”); it never invents a time. No names, no phone numbers, nothing else: it is a public screen in a public room.

  • A vehicle with no plate recorded says No plate recorded and goes by its ticket number.
  • A Ready car drops off the screen after a few hours (set under the same display settings), so one collected without its visit being closed does not sit there all day.
  • If management would rather not show full plates, Mask Licence Plates in the same settings shows only the last three characters — enough to find your own car, not enough to read anyone else's.
The address contains a private token. Do not post it anywhere public. If it leaks, a manager can change the token in the same setting and the old address stops working.

Emails they receive

Setup › Notifications

Email is the only channel. Every message is a template you can edit, and every send is recorded against the customer and the visit:

  • Booking confirmation, and reminders before the appointment.
  • Vehicle ready for collection.
  • Something is needed from the customer, or a delay.
  • Vehicle due for service.
Operational messages about a vehicle in your care are always sent. Marketing is the only kind that depends on consent, and an imported record with no answer recorded counts as unknown, not as a refusal.
Check whether email is actually going out. Under Settings › Notifications › Where customer email goes, the Send Email to Customers switch decides whether messages reach real customers or are held at a test address while you check the wording. While it is off, every send still looks successful on screen and is recorded — it simply went to the test address, not the customer. Look here first if a customer says they were never told.

When it looks wrong

Most of these are the platform doing its job rather than failing at it. Check here before escalating.

Common things, explained

Read the row, then the ticket

What you seeWhat it means

“One job at a time” when starting workWorking as intended. Finish or pause the job already on your clock.

Habits worth keeping

Every shift

  • Close visits the same day. Open visits distort the queue, the waiting times and tomorrow's availability.
  • Search before you create. A duplicate customer splits their history in two.
  • Never type a placeholder into a contact field. Blank is honest; fake is worse than nothing.
  • Pause honestly. Held time is counted against the hold-up, not the technician.
  • Give a reason on an override. You will be asked about it eventually, and the record is what answers for you.

Nothing in the manual matches that. Try a shorter word — “parts”, “queue”, “email”, “bay”.
